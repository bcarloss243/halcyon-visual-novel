## 1. THE WORLD

### The Cloche

A massive domed city built over flooded New Orleans. Not a glass bubble — a layered system of interlocking panels, atmospheric processors, and energy harvesting arrays. Originally constructed as a research campus and prototype facility for storm energy harvesting technology, it became a civilization by default after the Great Storm.

**Origin:** Commissioned ~2045 by a government-private consortium (DOE, DARPA, energy billionaires, tech founders, university presidents). Construction took ~6 years. The Great Storm hit ~2053–2054. The Cloche survived, harvested the storm's energy, and protected everyone inside. As the outside world collapsed — cascading infrastructure failures, climate refugees, political collapse — the Cloche became the only functioning city on the Gulf Coast.

**Present day:** ~2124. Approximately 70 years since sealing. Nobody alive remembers the outside world from personal experience. The founding generation is dead. Their children are elderly. Molly and Lola's generation is third or fourth generation. Population: ~120,000.

**Why New Orleans:** The Mississippi River delta provides massive freshwater access. The Gulf generates the most powerful and predictable storm systems in the Western Hemisphere. Existing infrastructure (levee systems, pumping stations, deep-water port) provided a foundation. The unique geology of the delta basin creates a natural concavity — a geological cradle — that the dome structure anchors into. Alaric's grandfather, a New Orleans native, made the case and won over the consortium.

**Engineering:** Designed for a 20-year operational lifespan with external maintenance and resupply. It's been running three times that long on closed-loop systems. Everything is patched, jury-rigged, and held together by successive chief engineers — each one pushing the systems a little harder.

**The Threshold:** The area immediately surrounding the dome is a kill zone of redirected wind, electrical discharge, and pressure differentials. The exterior surface channels storm energy, carrying enormous electrical charge during weather events. You don't need guards when the building itself will kill you if you touch it wrong.

**Entry/exit:** Originally four gates (cardinal directions), now reduced to one functioning gate. Opening requires a complex mechanical process — atmospheric pressure equalization across the threshold, like docking a spacecraft. Theoretically people can leave; practically, almost no one does.

**Storm harvesting:** Alaric's grandfather designed the Cloche as a passive shield. Alaric converted it to an active harvester — turning storms from threats into fuel. This made the city stronger and his daughter sicker. The electromagnetic signature changes when harvesting. The hum of machinery intensifies. The dome vibrates at frequencies Molly's gift is tuned to. Alaric made the Cloche louder.

**The Outside (keep mysterious):** The official narrative says it's uninhabitable wasteland. The Ag District whispers suggest otherwise. Professor Delacroix has evidence the outside world has recovered. What's out there: permanent wandering weather systems the size of continents ("Hyper-Canes"), massively elevated water levels (New Orleans is an island/archipelago), eerie silence (birds extinct, insects rare). Do NOT show the outside in the first game. The moment you show it, it becomes mundane.

### Philosophy of Governance

The government's goal isn't control — it's **Alleviation of Suffering.** They look at the terrifying reality outside and say: "Why should our citizens carry the burden of this horror? Let science carry it." Vapeur is their crowning achievement: fluoride in the water for existential dread. The tragedy is that it works for almost everyone, and the people it doesn't work for (like Molly) are treated as malfunctioning, not gifted.

## 2. THE WARDS

Six Wards, each mapped to real New Orleans geography. The Ward system emerged organically from the original project's departmental structure. Hidden hierarchy: Engineering at the top (they built the dome), Agriculture at the bottom (growing food categorized as "maintenance" not "innovation").

### Ironwork / The Forge

**Real NOLA mapping:** CBD and Warehouse District.
**Function:** Engineering and infrastructure. Power center. This is where the Cloche is maintained — storm harvesting technology, atmospheric processors, Vapeur distribution systems. Alaric's domain.
**Aesthetic:** Clean lines, humming machinery, data displays. Beautiful in a sterile way. Perfectly regulated air, optimal lighting. Everything works.
**Notable:** The Ward where the hum is loudest. Molly grew up here — the place that generates the exact frequency that torments her. Occupies the most structurally reinforced section of the dome, closest to the central support column.

### Inkwork / The Archives

**Real NOLA mapping:** Uptown academic corridor (Loyola/Tulane area).
**Function:** Knowledge and research. Libraries, laboratories, data servers, historical record. Preserves and advances human knowledge. Halcyon Academy sits at the border between the Archives and the Vieux Carré (symbolically: education between knowledge and culture).
**Aesthetic:** University campus that's been growing for seventy years. Libraries built on top of libraries. Quiet, contemplative, a little dusty in older sections.
**Notable:** This is where the official history lives — the curated, sanitized version of the founding. The Archives decide what gets remembered and what gets forgotten. That's power.

### Brasswork / Vieux Carré

**Real NOLA mapping:** The French Quarter.
**Function:** Cultural and commercial center. Preserves and evolves artistic traditions the founders believed were as essential as engineering. Jazz clubs, restaurants, wrought-iron balconies and neon. The pretty face of the Cloche.
**Aesthetic:** Holographic wrought iron, bioluminescent cobblestones. Molecular gastronomy alongside traditional Creole cooking. Jazz bands. Old money energy.
**Notable:** Exists because the founders made a deliberate choice — art is not optional. A civilization without culture isn't worth preserving. This is where the "morale and tourism" function lives. The pressure valve for human emotion.

### Bridgework / Tremé

**Real NOLA mapping:** Tremé — often called the birthplace of jazz, one of the oldest Black neighborhoods in America.
**Function:** Community, governance, labor. The connective tissue Ward — the people who actually make the city run day to day. Where the allocation system is administered, where policy is debated, where political power is brokered.
**Notable:** Historically the heart of Black cultural life. Under the dome that legacy persists in complicated ways. Second-line parades that happen daily. Drum circles. Music, gathering, resistance. This is where political savvy lives.

### Bayou Saint-Jean

**Real NOLA mapping:** Bayou St. John — beautiful, slightly mystical waterway neighborhood.
**Function:** Medical, wellness, and water management. Where the Vapeur clinics are likely concentrated. May also have a spiritual/cultural identity — where old folk traditions held on strongest in the Upper Cloche.
**Notable:** The least developed Ward in your worldbuilding. Potential for water management systems, spiritual identity, or both.

### The Ninth / Ag District

**Real NOLA mapping:** Lower 9th Ward and the Bayous.
**Function:** Agriculture, food production, biological systems maintenance. The essential work everyone depends on but nobody respects. Also preserves the Rootwork/Traiteur traditions that the Upper Cloche dismissed.
**Aesthetic:** Water-logged, lush, overgrown. Houses on stilts. Spanish moss (fiber-optic variant). Air that smells like wet earth. Physically located on the outer ring — closest to the gate, most exposed to the elements, always meant to be sacrificed first if the Cloche contracted.
**Notable:** Lola's home. The last people to be let in during the founding — they survived the storm and banged on the door. That's a fundamentally different relationship to the city than families like Molly's, who designed the door. The Ninth's contributions are assessed as "maintenance" not "innovation," so they receive baseline allocation while Forge engineers get bonuses.

## 3. ECONOMY

### Allocation System

No traditional currency. Resource allocation based on contribution assessments — the city evaluates how much value your work produces and rewards accordingly.

**Base allocation:** Every resident receives baseline automatically — housing, food, energy, Vapeur. Nobody starves. The system is genuinely generous at the floor.

**Supplementary allocation:** Better housing, premium food, priority scheduling, extra energy for personal projects. Earned through contribution assessments designed by the Forge and the Archives — measuring output, efficiency, innovation, intellectual contribution. Farming is categorized as maintenance. A Forge engineer who optimizes the energy grid by 2% gets a significant credit boost. A Ninth farmer who keeps 120,000 people alive gets baseline.

### Currency Terms

Three terms for three relationships to the same system:

- **Taps** — Primary citywide term. Physical and intuitive: everyone understands tapping a resource line because that's how everything works in a closed system. Connected to water, the lifeblood of the city. A tap can be opened or closed — someone controls the tap.
- **Lagniappe** — Supplementary credits above baseline. Louisiana French for "a little something extra." The word promises generosity; the system delivers hierarchy. The Ninth almost never earns lagniappe.
- **Gris-gris** — The Ninth's informal term for their credits. In Louisiana voodoo tradition, gris-gris are small charm bags carried for luck and protection. The Ninth treats their meager credits as protective charms rather than economic units. They're not saving up to get ahead. They're holding on to survive.

### Informal Economy

Favors and relationships are the real currency, especially in upper Wards. Food from the Vieux Carré is the closest thing to luxury goods. Three-generation Forge families have webs of relationships that translate into access. Ninth families have networks that get them better seeds and repair help, not Academy spots.

## 4. VAPEUR

The dominant wellness product. A mass-prescribed mood stabilizer that the city treats as a public health triumph.

**Origin:** Invented by Alaric's grandfather to treat his wife's condition (which was actually Weather Traiteur sensitivity, not mental illness). Extracted folk botanical knowledge into pharmaceutical form while discarding the spiritual framework.

**How it works in-game:**
- Taking Vapeur immediately sets pressure to 25 (comfortable zone)
- A hidden "vapeurDebt" variable accumulates the difference between actual and masked pressure
- The gauge cannot go below 20 while on Vapeur — Clarity zone is locked
- Wears off at end of day or next morning
- Rebound: pressure = previous_base + (vapeurDebt × 0.6). Severe.
- Consecutive day use increases the rebound multiplier
- Visual indicator: subtle purple tint on gauge glass while active

**Cultural role:** "Fluoride in the water." Almost everyone takes it. The slogan — Lola mimics it: "But they work on everyone! Literally everyone!" — is ubiquitous. Not taking Vapeur is seen as reckless, like refusing a vaccine. The system doesn't account for people it doesn't work on.

**The trap:** Vapeur is mechanically a trap the player is meant to gradually discover. Short-term relief, long-term escalation. The game teaches this through experience, not exposition.

## 5. MAGIC SYSTEM

### Rootwork & Traiteur Traditions

Based on real Louisiana traditions. Traiteurs ("treaters") are Cajun and Creole folk healers going back centuries. In the Cloche, the traditions are syncretically blended across lineages — historically grounded in New Orleans' actual Vodou/hoodoo/rootwork/Cajun Catholic folk healing history. You don't need to invent a justification for the traditions being swirled together — New Orleans already did that over centuries.

### Molly's Gift: Weather Traiteur

Molly has atmospheric weather sensitivity inherited from her paternal grandmother. She can feel barometric pressure changes, approaching storms, and atmospheric conditions through the dome walls. This has been pathologized as anxiety disorder her entire life. Vapeur — designed to treat exactly this condition — doesn't work on her because there's nothing to treat. It's not a disease. It's a gift.

**The hum:** What Molly hears is the dome's resonant frequencies interacting with storm energy. At low pressure, it's background noise. At high pressure, it's deafening. The hum has been her constant companion and her greatest source of suffering. In the climax, it stops being noise and resolves into something she can work with.

### Lola's Gift: Terrestrial/Earth

Complementary to Molly's atmospheric gift. Earth, soil, biological systems. Fire and earth to Molly's water and air. She personifies the natural world — "The soil in sector seven is tired today. Like it's been holding its breath." In the Ninth this is normal. At the Academy, it's considered quirky at best.

### The Climax

Molly's gift doesn't activate through training. It activates because she's standing at the edge of the Ninth watching the water rise and Lola is on the other side and every rational system says this is acceptable losses and something inside her says no. She stops fighting the connection to the weather and surrenders to it. The Traiteur tradition isn't about controlling nature — it's about asking. Molly asks the storm to turn. The storm — which has been trying to reach her through the dome walls her entire life — hears her.

## 6. CHARACTERS

### Molly (Protagonist)

Weather Traiteur whose gift has been pathologized by the Vapeur establishment. Daughter of Alaric. Grew up in the Forge — the loudest Ward for her condition. Every doctor, every chemist, every diagnostic tool says she has treatment-resistant anxiety. She's not sick. She's hearing the weather.

**Relationship with Alaric:** Volatile. He controls her academic track (locked Greenwork, pushed Ironwork). He keeps upping her Vapeur dosage. Every decision motivated by love and terror — he's watching his mother happen again inside his daughter.

### Alaric (Father)

Chief Engineer of the Cloche. Third-generation inheritor. Tragic rather than villainous — every decision motivated by love for Molly, but his "science explains everything" framework prevents him from seeing her clearly.

**Generational arc:** His grandfather was the visionary who understood the storm. His father was the builder who respected the storm. Alaric thinks he's conquered the storm. He's wrong. He overclocked the harvester to prove he wasn't just a caretaker — he was an innovator. In doing so he broke what his grandfather designed to be unbreakable, and made the Cloche louder in exactly the frequency range that torments his daughter.

**His mother:** Weather Traiteur like Molly. Pathologized, medicated, called crazy. Alaric grew up watching her deteriorate under a diagnosis that never fit. The massive storm that killed people outside the dome broke her completely — the Vapeur couldn't stop her from feeling that destruction. Alaric never knew his mother before she went "insane." His entire career is a reaction to this.

**The crisis:** He will order the sacrifice of the Ag District to save the rest of the Cloche when the unimaginable storm hits. Not out of malice — out of math. The math has always said the Ninth is expendable. That's why it was built where it was built.

### Lola

Halcyon's only BioAg student. From the Ninth Ward. Magnetically present, funny, charming as a survival strategy. Her ease conceals the weight of leaving her ailing grandmother (Mémère).

**Mémère:** Family matriarch. Rootwork practitioner of the old tradition. Aging and increasingly frail. Lola was her primary caretaker before the Academy. The guilt of leaving is constant and she never talks about it.

**Character details:** Code-switches fluidly (Academy diction in seminars, full Ninth vernacular with Molly once trust is built). Talks to plants — actually talks to them. Collects strange things (bone shaped like an unknown letter, contraband dome wall fragment, jars of soil labeled with dates and feelings). Does devastating impressions of every professor. Gives everything a name — her favorite greenhouse plant is Gerald. Dances alone in the greenhouse to Bayou music only she hears. Art minor.

**Relationship with Molly:** Built on mutual presence, not rescue. Deliberately distinguished from the Elphaba/Glinda archetype. The first time Lola code-switches around Molly — talks to her the way she talks at home — is a trust milestone.

### Molly's Mother

Has intuition but not knowledge about Molly's condition. Not from the Ninth — doesn't know about Rootwork or Traiteur traditions. She watched her mother-in-law and sees the echo in Molly. She doesn't think "Weather Traiteur." She thinks "something about this feels familiar and it scares me." May have subtly steered Molly toward Halcyon Academy, knowing the greenhouse existed.

### The Professors (AI-Powered Mentors)

Each major track has a lead professor using LLM integration (Claude API). 3–5 exchanges per visit. Conversations saved and persistent. Fallback scripted dialogue for offline/API failure.

**Professor Marchand (Ironwork):** Veteran Forge engineer. Contemporary of Alaric. Speaks in measurements and specifications. Sees Molly's "anxiety" as medical. Dismissive of Rootwork — not cruel, just no framework. *Hidden depth:* Present when Alaric overclocked the harvesting system. Raised concerns. Was overruled. Carries guilt.

**Professor Thibodaux (Greenwork/BioAg):** Ag District elder. Moves between scientific language and Rootwork traditions carefully. Knows which knowledge is safe to share institutionally. First to hint Molly's symptoms aren't illness but gift — never directly until she's ready. *Hidden depth:* Knew Molly's mother. Has been watching Molly her entire life, waiting to see if the gift surfaced.

**Professor Delacroix (Inkwork):** Historian obsessed with accuracy and completeness. Spent his career compiling the real founding history, including suppressed parts. Not radical — just believes truth matters. Will leave doors unlocked without explicitly breaking rules. *Hidden depth:* Has evidence the outside world has recovered. Doesn't know what to do with it.

**Professor Beaumont (Brasswork):** Musician and cultural historian. Teaches through stories and songs. Believes the Cloche's soul is its culture. Most likely to validate Molly's emotional experience: "Baby, feeling things deeply isn't a diagnosis. It's how the best music gets written." *Hidden depth:* Composes in secret. Her recent compositions include frequencies that seem to soothe the hum. She doesn't know why.

**Professor Fontenot (Bridgework):** Tremé governance expert. Practical, politically savvy, somewhat cynical. Teaches how power actually flows — not how it's supposed to. Arms Molly with political tools but counsels patience over revolution. *Hidden depth:* Has been quietly documenting Ward inequality for years, building a case she's never had political support to present.

## 7. ACADEMIC TRACKS

Alaric locked Greenwork as a major for Molly. She can minor in it, sneak visits to the greenhouse, build relationships through free time — but she can't officially be a Greenwork student. The locked option should haunt the player.

Each track gives Molly distinct capabilities affecting daily life and the climax:

- **Ironwork:** Technical knowledge. Can read Forge diagnostics, understand dome warnings, speak Alaric's language. Climax: propose engineering solutions that redirect power without sacrificing the Ninth entirely.
- **Inkwork:** Historical knowledge and research skills. Can find suppressed documents, uncover founding triage records, build evidence-based arguments. Climax: expose the political history of the Ninth's expendability.
- **Brasswork:** Cultural connection and community influence. Knows people across Wards through performance/events. Climax: mobilize people, organize evacuation, lead a second line to safety.
- **Bridgework:** Political and governance knowledge. Understands allocation system, power structure, legal frameworks. Climax: challenge Alaric's authority through institutional channels.
- **Greenwork (always the minor):** The Rootwork connection. The real power. A Molly who switches to Greenwork can speak to the storm directly. She becomes the halcyon: calm in the midst of chaos.

Late-game option to defy Alaric and formally switch to Greenwork should be one of the most consequential choices in the game.

## 8. GREENHOUSE PLANTS

Invented names drawing on Louisiana geography, Creole French, and folk herbalism:

- **Lumière Moss** — Bioluminescent Spanish moss variant, glows faintly blue
- **Bâton Rouge Fern** — Deep red fern with veins that pulse when absorbing water
- **Nénuphar Dormant** — Water lily that only opens during artificial night cycles
- **Sassafras Volant** — Airborne root system that grows upward
- **Pétale de Cuivre** — "Copper petal," blooms are metallic, between organic and mineral
- **Verveine Noire** — "Black vervain," dark-leafed medicinal herb from hoodoo tradition that Vapeur chemists don't take seriously
- **Chandeleur Vine** — Named for the destroyed Chandeleur Islands, feeds off greenhouse liquid tubes
- **Jardin Creux** — "Hollow garden," interconnected root systems sharing nutrients — looks like separate plants but is one organism
- **Fleur de Sel** — Salt-tolerant flowering succulent, important for salinity problems in flooded New Orleans agriculture
- **Tremblante** — "The trembling one," reacts to sound/vibration, folds leaves when you speak near it. Reacts to Molly's presence in a way that surprises Lola — a quiet device for hinting at Molly's gift before anyone understands it

## 9. AUDIO: THE HUM

Three layered audio tracks mapping to Pressure value:

**Layer 1 — Low Drone (always present):** Deep, barely perceptible bass tone. At low pressure, felt more than heard. At high pressure, prominent and unsettling. Volume maps to pressure (0.1 at 0, 0.8 at 100). Pitch shifts lower as pressure increases.

**Layer 2 — Static/Interference (mid-to-high):** Crackling, electrical interference. Absent at low pressure. Fades in above pressure 40. Dominant above 70.

**Layer 3 — Signal (low pressure only):** Something almost musical that emerges when the static clears. Only audible in Clarity zone. This is the sound of Molly's gift working — the weather trying to communicate. Most players won't hear it often, which makes it precious.

## 10. KEY NARRATIVE BEATS

**The Founding Triage:** When the Great Storm hit, people outside survived and found the Cloche. Leadership had to decide who got in. The official history says they accepted everyone they could. The real story — who was left outside, who decided — is buried. The Ninth remembers. Some Ninthers' oral histories include stories of hearing people outside banging on the walls.

**The Storm Power Reveal:** Only visible if the player is paying attention or chose the Ironwork track. When the Cloche harvests storms, the lights/power buzz or glow brighter for a moment. On the engineering track, Molly can access the power grid and see the correlation between harvesting intensity and her symptoms.

**The Alaric Choice:** Alaric orders the Ninth sacrificed when the unimaginable storm hits. Molly is held back. Lola is screaming, crying for Molly to do something. The player's accumulated choices, relationships, and knowledge determine what options are available. A player who spent all their time in the Forge and never visited the Ninth might not realize what's being sacrificed until too late.

**The Gift Activates:** Not through training — through love. Molly stops fighting the hum and surrenders to it. The storm responds because she finally stopped blocking it out and started listening.

## 11. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

- **"Bad days are more valuable"** — The game rewards engaging with difficulty
- **It's a perfect city for very imperfect people** — It solves for weather, logistics, infrastructure but cannot solve grief, loneliness, longing
- **The ultimate tragedy:** No system can save people from being people
- **The generational arc:** Visionary → Builder → Inheritor. The grandfather understood the storm. The father respected the storm. The son thinks he's conquered it.
- **The locked path IS the key** — The one thing Alaric forbids is the one thing that could save everyone
