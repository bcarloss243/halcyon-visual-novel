# Halcyon Academy — Core Design Document
## Version 3.0 — Comprehensive
## Bergen Carloss | Updated March 2026

---

## THE GAME IN THREE SENTENCES

You manage a young woman's daily life at an academy inside a domed city, choosing how she spends her time while watching a Pressure Gauge that you think measures her anxiety but secretly measures something much bigger.

You progress by building relationships, uncovering your family's hidden history, and developing abilities that the city's science says don't exist — all leading to an impossible choice during a catastrophic storm.

You come back because the gauge remembers yesterday, the people remember what you did for them, and the hum in Molly's head gets a little clearer every time you choose the greenhouse over the Vapeur.

## PART ONE: THE WORLD

### The Cloche

A massive dome built over the flooded remains of New Orleans. Originally a government/private consortium project (Project Halcyon) to develop storm energy harvesting technology. The dome was designed to anchor into the natural basin of the Mississippi Delta — the physics demanded New Orleans specifically.

Before the dome, a catastrophic flood drove most of the population out. Roughly 15,000 holdouts refused to leave — people whose identity was inseparable from the land, including Rootwork practitioners whose spiritual traditions required the specific soil, water, and air of the Delta. The consortium built the dome around and over these holdouts.

The great founding storm sealed the Cloche permanently. The outside world collapsed. The proof of concept became the last city. 70+ years later, the population of ~120,000 lives in a Venice-style canal city under the dome, organized into Wards.

Inside the Cloche, the weather is climate-controlled. Temperate and sunny every day regardless of what's happening outside. Citizens never feel storms. The only visible sign of external weather is a brief brightening of lights when the storm harvesting system engages. Only Forge engineers monitoring the power grid know when storms actually hit.

### The Wards

**The Forge (Engineering)** — Power center. Where the dome is maintained, storm harvesting operated, atmospheric processors managed. Alaric's Ward. Clean lines, humming machinery, perfectly regulated air. The hum is loudest here. Molly grew up here and it makes her sicker than anywhere else in the city.

**The Archives (Knowledge/History)** — Intellectual center. Libraries, laboratories, data servers, historical records. Where the official history lives — curated, sanitized. Also where suppressed documents about the founding triage can be found if you know where to look.

**The Vieux Carré (Arts/Culture)** — Cultural heart. Music, performance, visual arts, culinary arts. Wrought-iron balconies over neon-lit canals. Jazz clubs, floating restaurants, street musicians. Art isn't decoration here — it's infrastructure. The Vapeur keeps people chemically stable. The art keeps them spiritually alive.

**Tremé (Governance/Community)** — Civic life. Law, resource allocation, public health, Vapeur clinics. Where you go when you need help, not innovation. Manages the human side of the city.

**Bayou Saint-Jean (Medical/Life Sciences)** — Medicine, pharmacology, Vapeur development and distribution. Sits between inner Wards and the Ninth. Some researchers here have more contact with the Ninth than most Upper Cloche residents.

**The Ninth (Ag District)** — Outer ring. Where the dome meets the earth. Stilted houses, wild bayou water, Spanish moss, bioluminescent insects. Less processed air, more humidity, plants growing wilder. The only Ward where people touch actual soil. Where Rootwork practitioners live. Where the oral histories of the founding are kept. Where the holdouts' descendants carry knowledge the Upper Cloche can't perceive.

**Halcyon Academy** — Sits at the nexus where Archives, Vieux Carré, and Tremé meet. First cross-Ward educational institution. Alaric's creation, designed to train the next generation of Ward leaders and position Molly as his successor. Small first class of 20-30 students from all Wards. The one place where Ward boundaries blur.

### Navigation

The Cloche is a Venice-style canal city. Boats are the primary transportation — and they vary by Ward. Riverboat-style vessels in the Vieux Carré. Industrial barges in the Forge. Pirogues, airboats, and flat-bottomed fishing boats in the Ninth. What someone rides tells you where they're from.

### The Vapeur

Invented by Alaric's father as a prototype medication for one patient — his wife (Alaric's mother), who had the same Weather Traiteur gift that Molly would later inherit. The Vapeur was designed to quiet the neural pathways causing her "episodes." It worked partially — enough to be repurposed as general-population medication when the Cloche sealed and 80,000 people needed treatment for dome-anxiety.

**What Vapeur actually is:** A genuine, helpful medication comparable to SSRIs. It treats the legitimate psychological toll of living in an enclosed environment. It doesn't zombify people or suppress creativity. Citizens on Vapeur are productive, emotional, creative, intelligent. It just makes the existential load bearable.

**What Vapeur can't do:** It cannot push Molly into the blue/clarity zone. It caps her perception. She can be stable on Vapeur but never access her gift. The medication treats the symptom (anxiety) while blocking the channel her abilities use.

**Why Molly is different:** The Vapeur doesn't fully work on her because her anxiety isn't purely psychological — it's her Weather Traiteur gift interpreting atmospheric data through the same neural pathways the Vapeur is trying to quiet. The medication is fighting her biology.

### The Three-Generation Tragedy

**Alaric's grandfather:** Visionary. New Orleans native who convinced the consortium to build here because the physics demanded it. Designed the Cloche as a passive shield — protective, sturdy, humble before the storms.

**Alaric's father:** Builder. Managed the founding crisis. Made the triage decisions about who got in. Invented the Vapeur to save his wife (Alaric's mother), who had the Weather Traiteur gift. She shattered during the founding storm — felt the people dying outside, banging on the walls. The Vapeur couldn't touch that grief. She broke and never recovered. Alaric was a small child. He never knew his mother before the madness.

**Alaric:** Inheritor. Grew up watching his mother suffer from something he couldn't understand. Chose his father's framework — science, engineering, control — over his mother's reality. Overclocked the passive dome into an active storm harvester to generate more power. Every innovation made the Cloche louder in the exact frequency range that Molly's gift is tuned to. He's literally making his daughter sicker by improving the machine, and he doesn't know it because his framework says weather and anxiety are in different categories.

**Molly's mother:** Present. Professionally accomplished (researcher or governance role). Has intuition about Molly's condition — recognizes the echo of Alaric's mother — but lacks the framework to name it. She's not withholding the answer. She's standing right next to it in the dark, reaching for a light switch that was never installed. Her love manifests as worry, as presence, as small gestures (not objecting to Molly's interest in plants) without ever being able to explain why.

## PART TWO: THE CHARACTERS

### Molly Duvernay

Protagonist. Daughter of the Cloche's architect. Carries the Weather Traiteur gift inherited from her grandmother (Alaric's mother), skipping a generation. Experiences a constant "hum" — atmospheric data interpreted as static because the Cloche's machinery distorts the signal. Has genuine anxiety independent of the gift, exacerbated by it.

Water and air. Fluid, shifting, sometimes drowning. On good Pressure days she's sharp, curious, perceptive, quietly funny. On bad days the world is static and noise. The player experiences both through the Pressure Gauge.

### Lola Landry

Molly's counterpart. From the Ninth. BioAg prodigy with a Rootwork gift — she feels the earth the way Molly feels the air. Touches plants and knows what they need. Walks on the outer ring and feels structural vibrations through the ground.

Fire and earth. Magnetic, warm, funny, tactile. Always touching things — leaves, soil, seeds in her pocket. Talks to plants. Collects strange things. Draws obsessive botanical illustrations mixing scientific terminology and Rootwork language. Treats her Academy uniform as a suggestion — pins on the lapel, rolled sleeves, a handmade bag from the Ninth covered in Mémère's embroidered patches.

Her charm is strategic — she learned that being likable is protection. The flirtation with boys is armor. She code-switches between Academy diction and Ninth vernacular. Molly is the first person she drops the performance for.

Carries guilt about leaving the Ninth and Mémère, who needs daily physical care. Her secret desire is to bridge Rootwork and science — build something neither tradition could build alone. The Academy is slowly convincing her that her intuitive knowledge is "just" good observation skills. Molly's existence disproves that.

Art minor. Her sketchbook is the most honest document of her internal life — botanical illustrations that accidentally become maps of root systems, Academy courtyards that transform into bayous.

### Alaric

Molly's father. The Cloche's architect and chief engineer. Brilliant, driven, emotionally guarded. Everything he does is for Molly — the harvesting system, the Vapeur refinements, the Academy, the locked Greenwork major. All acts of love. All making her worse.

His epistemological bias isn't bigotry — it's the inability to perceive what Rootwork practitioners experience. A colorblind person running a society and concluding that people who claim to see red are mentally ill. He applies this same framework to his own daughter and it's destroying her and he's the last person who will understand why.

At the core, he's a dad who would rebuild the entire dome for his daughter and can't sit in the dark and listen to her. The player should ache for him, not hate him.

### Mémère

Lola's grandmother. Family matriarch. Rootwork practitioner of the old depth — the last person holding the complete understanding of the tradition. Aging, increasingly frail, needs daily care. Her knowledge is inseparable from the soil. When she dies, a lineage narrows to whatever Lola absorbed during childhood mornings in the bayou.

### The Professors

**Professor Marchand (Ironwork):** Veteran Forge engineer. Loyal to the dome's systems. Genuinely cares about Molly but sees her symptoms as medical. Was present when Alaric overclocked the harvester. Raised concerns. Was overruled. Carries guilt.

**Professor Thibodaux (Greenwork/BioAg):** Ag District elder. Moves carefully between scientific language and Rootwork traditions. Knew Alaric's mother. Has been watching Molly her entire life, waiting to see if the gift surfaced. The most important mentor in the game — but only if the player builds enough trust.

**Professor Delacroix (Inkwork):** Historian. Obsessed with completeness. Has evidence the outside world recovered. Doesn't know what to do with it. Will leave doors unlocked without breaking rules.

**Professor Beaumont (Brasswork):** Musician and cultural historian. Teaches through stories and songs. Validates emotional experience without pathologizing it. Composes music that accidentally soothes the hum — frequencies that bridge the Forge's science and the Ninth's Rootwork without her understanding why.

**Professor Fontenot (Bridgework):** Governance expert. Practical, politically savvy. Teaches how power actually flows. Has been quietly documenting Ward inequality for years.

## PART THREE: THE PRESSURE GAUGE

### Visual Design

An Art Deco brass barometric instrument with Molly's portrait centered inside. Always visible in the lower-right corner. Steam vents at the top. Wrought-iron ornamental base. Fleur-de-lis accent. "HALCYON" etched at the bottom.

The gauge is the game's visual signature — as recognizable as Citizen Sleeper's dice.

### The Four Zones

| Zone | Range | Color | Experience |
|------|-------|-------|-----------|
| Crisis (Red) | 70-100 | Deep crimson | Hum deafening. Can barely function. Risk of panic attack. BUT: Rootwork practice more effective, relationship deepening amplified, perceptive insights unlocked. Bad days are more valuable for growth. |
| Elevated (Amber) | 45-70 | Burnished gold | Hum loud. Everything costs more. Pushing through. Where most of early game is spent. |
| Manageable (Green) | 20-45 | Verdigris | Hum is background noise. Functional. Productive. Where Vapeur locks you. |
| Clarity (Blue) | 0-20 | Deep teal | Rare. Hum resolves into signal. Perceptive gifts activate. Cannot reach while on Vapeur. |

### Morning Pressure Determination

Four inputs — three visible, one hidden:

- **Relationship state** (visible): Previous day's social interactions
- **Vapeur status** (visible): Medicated? Rebound pending?
- **Physical rest** (visible): Did player push too hard yesterday?
- **Weather outside the dome** (hidden): Approaching storms raise baseline. Player cannot see or control this. The invisible hand of anxiety.
- **Random variance** (±8): Some days are just harder.

### Morning Routine Window

Before the day starts, small choices that nudge the gauge 5-10%:

- Take Vapeur (drops to green, but caps at green, can't reach blue)
- Sit with the hum (no immediate benefit, trains long-term tolerance)
- Look at the canal (small calming effect)
- Read a message from Lola (small calming effect if relationship is strong)
- Skip (saves time, no adjustment)

### The Vapeur System (Revised — Medication as Genuinely Helpful)

**Consistent use helps.** Daily Vapeur stabilizes morning Pressure baseline. Highs less high, lows less low. Functional, productive, stable. This is a genuine benefit. The game never frames this as weakness.

**Skipping doses hurts.** Abrupt discontinuation after consistent use causes withdrawal spikes — worse than never taking it. Just like real SSRIs. The game teaches: if you're going to taper off Vapeur, do it gradually, with support (Thibodaux's guidance).

**The ceiling, not the trap.** Vapeur caps at green. Blue/clarity is inaccessible while medicated. Consistent Vapeur gives Molly a good, stable, functional life. It locks the Weather Traiteur ending but doesn't ruin the game — it means Molly faces the storm as a leader or a lover rather than as a Traiteur.

**The choice is real, not obvious.** Staying on Vapeur = safe, stable, two endings available. Tapering off with support = volatile, harder, all three endings available. Going cold turkey = dangerous withdrawal, the game punishes recklessness not courage.

### The Microclimate Effect

Relationships don't fix Pressure. They become places where the weather is gentler.

Sheltering moments — choosing to spend time with someone on a high-Pressure day — build a "microclimate" value for that person. When Molly is near someone with a high microclimate value, Pressure increases are softened and decreases are amplified.

Showing up on bad days builds the relationship faster than showing up on good days. Vulnerability deepens bonds more than competence.

Lola's microclimate is the strongest. Alaric's can go negative after conflict — his presence amplifies Pressure changes.

### Audio: The Hum

Three layered audio tracks mapped to Pressure:

- **Layer 1 — Low Drone** (always present): Deep bass tone. Louder and heavier at high Pressure.
- **Layer 2 — Static** (mid-to-high): Crackling interference. Fades in above 40. Dominant above 70.
- **Layer 3 — Signal** (low Pressure only): Faint, almost musical tone. Only audible below 25. The sound of the gift working correctly. As Rootwork develops, this layer begins appearing at higher Pressure values.

Storm harvest events spike all layers with a distinct electrical buzz and brief light brightening.

## PART FOUR: DAILY LIFE AND TIME STRUCTURE

### Quarter-Based Academic Calendar

The game runs on an academic quarter system — approximately 10-11 weeks (70-75 in-game days) per quarter. Chapter 1 covers the first quarter. Each subsequent chapter covers a quarter, with Chapter 5 (the storm) potentially interrupting the final quarter.

This time-bound structure creates natural tension: every day spent resting is a day not studying, not deepening relationships, not pursuing Rootwork. The impossible triangle — self-care vs. performance vs. passion — IS the game.

### Academic Milestones

Each quarter requires minimum thresholds in the player's chosen track. Miss too many classes or neglect academic work and consequences escalate:

1. **Warning dispatch** — Formal, bureaucratic. "Your performance has fallen below acceptable standards."
2. **Alaric finds out** — The real consequence. Dinner where his disappointment leaks through. He increases Vapeur dosage. Suggests she stop visiting the greenhouse.
3. **Academic probation** — Formally notified. Fewer activity slots. Mandatory remedial work eating into free time. Alaric's oversight tightens.

**Molly cannot drop out.** She's the architect's daughter. The system bends before it breaks for her. That's privilege functioning as a cage — she can't even fail on her own terms.

### Daily Structure

**Morning Block — Classes (mandatory)**
Based on major/minor track. Types: Lectures (passive, low energy), Labs/Workshops (active, moderate energy), Field Studies (travel to Ward, high energy), Seminars (discussion, requires moderate Pressure to participate well).

**Afternoon Block — Free Time (player's choice)**
- Visit Lola / Visit a friend
- Explore a Ward (by boat — the journey is part of the experience)
- Visit your Mentor
- Study / Research
- Work a Side Job (earn Taps)
- Rest (reliable Pressure reduction, never punished)
- Take Vapeur
- Practice Rootwork (only after Lola introduces it; more effective on high-Pressure days)

**Evening Block — Personal**
- Write in journal (player types freely; saved as Molly's voice)
- Listen to music (Ward-specific radio stations)
- Contact Alaric
- Canal walk
- Message Lola or a friend
- Sleep (ends day)

### Weekly Rhythm

- **Academy days (5):** Full schedule
- **Market Day:** No classes. Canal markets. Best for exploration and social time.
- **Rest Day:** Minimal options. The Cloche is quiet. Hum is clearest. Good for long Ninth visits or recovery.

### Choosing a Track

**Ironwork** — Engineering. Alaric's preferred path. High Pressure cost (Forge is loudest). Climax ability: Engineering solutions, challenge Alaric's calculations.

**Inkwork** — Archives/History. Pressure-neutral. Climax ability: Expose founding triage history, rally support through truth.

**Brasswork** — Arts/Culture. Low Pressure cost. Climax ability: Mobilize people, lead a Second Line through crisis.

**Bridgework** — Governance. Moderate Pressure cost. Climax ability: Challenge Alaric through institutional channels.

**Greenwork — LOCKED AS MAJOR.** Grayed out on selection screen. "Per parental recommendation, this concentration is not available as a primary track." Available as minor only. The real power lives in the minor — Rootwork, the gift, the Weather Traiteur path. The locked path IS the key.

### The Major Switch (Late Game)

Option to formally switch major to Greenwork. Costs: devastates Alaric relationship, some Upper Cloche relationships cool, academic standing in former major lost. Unlocks: full Rootwork training, accelerated gift development, the third ending.

### The Dispatch System

2-3 context-sensitive messages each morning from characters. Not quest markers — people talking to Molly naturally. Lola's are warm and specific. Alaric's are concerned and slightly controlling. Thibodaux's are careful and coded. During storm escalation, messages shift in tone — shorter, more urgent, more desperate. When the Ninth loses communication, Lola's messages stop. The silence is the loudest thing in the game.

### The Journal

Molly's private space. Accumulates automatically (sketches of new locations, notes from class) and through player input (typed entries saved as Molly's voice in the player's words). No Pressure adjustment, no strategic value. Just expression. Visual style: handmade notebook, pressed plants, ink smudges, Lola's doodles in margins once relationship is deep enough.

## PART FIVE: THE FAIL STATES

### No Traditional Game Over

Halcyon never shows a "game over" screen. Failure is expressed through emotional consequence, not mechanical termination.

### Panic Attack (Soft Fail)

Pressure exceeds 95: day ends early, remaining activities lost. Next day starts with limited options — morning block locked, low-energy activities only. Effectively costs 1.5 days. Characters notice the absence — dispatches express concern. Repeated panic attacks compound: Lola's messages get more worried then less frequent. Academic standing drops. Alaric increases Vapeur without asking.

### Vapeur Dependency (Not Punished — Capped)

Consistent Vapeur use is genuinely helpful but caps Molly at green. Player who stays on Vapeur has a stable, functional playthrough with two endings available. Not a fail state — a safe choice with a ceiling. The Weather Traiteur ending is locked.

### Relationship Decay

Neglecting Lola doesn't remove her from the game — the greenhouse meeting is scripted. But relationship depth determines which endings are available and how devastating the climax feels. Shallow relationship = two endings, less emotional weight. Deep relationship = potential for all three endings, maximum emotional impact.

### The Inaction Ending (Hidden 4th Ending)

If the player freezes during the climax — runs out of time, fails to act — the system's default triage activates. The Ninth floods automatically. Worse than Ending 1 because nobody chose it. The architecture chose. The system did what it was always designed to do. Molly has to live with the knowledge that her inability to choose resulted in the worst outcome for everyone.

## PART SIX: THE COMIC BOOK AESTHETIC

### Why Comics

Comics exist in the space between moments — the reader's imagination fills the motion between panels. That's how anxiety works: the worst part is the anticipation, the gutter between what's happening and what's about to happen. Molly's life is the space between panels. Comics make that space visible.

Comics also give the player control of emotional tempo. They linger on panels at their own pace. The greenhouse meeting as a sequence of panels is intimate and private in a way animation cannot be.

The aesthetic is authentic to New Orleans visual culture — Mardi Gras poster art, jazz album covers, hand-painted signs, narrative beadwork. The Ninth's culture IS visual storytelling tradition.

### How It Works

**Daily gameplay** — Illustrated backgrounds, character portraits, Pressure Gauge, activity cards, dispatches, dialogue. The Citizen Sleeper layer. Interface-driven, functional, beautiful.

**Key story moments** — Shift into comic panel sequences. 2-6 panels per page. Player advances at their own pace. Subtle motion possible (parallax, particles, gauge needle twitching) without full animation.

### Panel Style Shifts by Ward

- **Forge scenes:** Sharp lines, cool colors (blues, silvers), precise architectural panel borders. Drawn by an engineer.
- **Ninth scenes:** Loose, warm linework, organic colors (greens, golds, browns), irregular hand-drawn borders. Drawn in a Rootwork journal.
- **Vieux Carré scenes:** Most saturated and lush. Neon purples and teals bleeding outside borders. Jazz poster energy.
- **Academy scenes:** Between Forge and Vieux Carré. Greenhouse scenes lean toward Ninth style.

The player unconsciously associates visual style with emotional geography.

### Chapter 1 Comic Sequences

~25-35 panels across key scenes:
- Molly's arrival at the Academy (2-3 panels)
- Track selection with locked Greenwork (1-2 panels)
- The panic attack and greenhouse discovery (6-8 panels — the most lush sequence)
- First dinner with Alaric (2-3 panels)
- Chapter ending — gauge settling to green alone (1-2 panels)

### Art as Healing System

Art is the third healing pathway:
- Vapeur: chemical, instant, borrowed, caps at green
- Rootwork: spiritual/natural, slow, transformative, opens blue
- Art: immediate, honest, temporary but real — genuine relief without rebound or ceiling

Attending music/performance events lowers Pressure through beauty rather than medicine or magic. Professor Beaumont's compositions accidentally contain frequencies that bridge Forge science and Ninth Rootwork.

Each Ward has its own music in the ambient soundscape. When Molly moves between Wards, the music changes. The player HEARS the cultural difference. The Forge has no music — only machinery. That absence tells you everything about what Alaric values.

### Climax Panel Design

The final choice scene — full-page panels. Close-ups of hands, eyes, the gauge. Lola's scream breaks panel borders. Alaric's composure contained in rigid frames.

When Molly chooses:
- **Triage (Ending 1):** Art locks into sharp, cold, perfect lines
- **Save the Ninth (Ending 2):** Panels crack, go loose and organic
- **Weather Traiteur (Ending 3):** Panel borders dissolve, styles merge — Forge precision and Ninth warmth bleeding together

The art style becomes the ending.

## PART SEVEN: THE CLIMAX

### The Storm

Molly's Pressure has been rising for weeks. The storm approaching outside is massive — far beyond what Alaric's instruments indicate. His overclocked harvesting system is amplifying the structural stress. Engineers have raised concerns about harmonic vibrations. Alaric dismissed them.

Molly can feel the storm's true size because her gift reads its heart, not its leading edge. She and Lola go to Alaric. He can't hear them. He speaks Architecture. She speaks Barometric Pressure.

### The Room

The Forge's control room. Alaric at the controls. Lola restrained at the doorway — she came to warn them, unauthorized, held back by security following procedure. Molly between them.

Alaric: "All of this was for you. I built a city of progress, shields to protect you, systems to power your future... The accumulation of millions of years of evolution, of sacrifice, of genius. The finest society to ever exist. It can't be compromised. And it doesn't have to be."

Lola, through sobs: "Please! You can't do this! My family! The Ninth! You're one of us too! Molly, you know them! They love you! I love you. I've never loved anyone more than I love you. You have to do something."

The Pressure Gauge is screaming. The hum underneath their voices is resolving — not into static, not into music — into a question: "I'm here. I've always been here. What do you want to do?"

### The Four Endings

**Ending 1 — Sacrifice the Ninth:**
Molly supports the triage. Inner city protected. The Ninth floods. People die — Mémère, neighbors, elders carrying irreplaceable Rootwork knowledge. Lola survives physically but goes catatonic — the girl who talked to plants is gone. Molly ascends to leadership. Gauge settles to permanent green. The hum goes quiet. Not because she learned to hear through it — because the thing it was trying to tell her about drowned.

**Ending 2 — Save the Ninth:**
Molly redirects power to the outer ring. Ninth survives. Flood water trickles into mechanical systems. Archives flood. Forge goes dark. Vapeur production stops. Everything goes dark. For the first time in 70 years, the entire population feels what Molly has felt her whole life. The illusion of perfect safety shatters.

**Ending 3 — Weather Traiteur:**
Only available if: player switched to Greenwork major, developed Rootwork deeply, built profound bond with Lola. Molly reaches through the dome wall and negotiates with the storm. Saves everything. But sees through the dome for the first time — feels the outside world, feels the echo of the founding storm, feels what her grandmother felt. Can never see her father the same way. Can never unknow what the Cloche cost.

**Ending 4 — Inaction (Hidden):**
Player freezes or runs out of time. Default triage activates automatically. Ninth floods catastrophically — worse than Ending 1. Nobody chose this. The system did what it was designed to do. The architecture sacrificed the Ninth without anyone's fingerprints on it.

### The Morning After (Playable Epilogue)

One final playable day after the storm. The player wakes up and lives in the consequence.

**After Ending 1:** Gauge reads perfect green. Activity cards pristine. City functioning. Greenhouse card gone. Lola's name absent. The player clicks through a perfect, quiet day in a smaller world. The silence where the music used to be.

**After Ending 2:** UI is broken — glitching, stripped of data. The gauge is erratic. Activities are limited. But the hum audio layer has changed. It's not static anymore. It's almost music. The system is broken but the signal is clearer than it's ever been.

**After Ending 3:** Everything looks normal. Gauge works. City functions. But Molly sees differently — faint weather data overlaid on everything, atmospheric readings that weren't there before. She walks through a normal day with abnormal perception. The gift is fully open. She can't turn it off.

**After Ending 4:** The worst morning. The city is damaged. The Ninth is devastated. And nobody is responsible. Molly walks through a world that was broken by default. The gauge reads green because the machine decided everything is fine.

## PART EIGHT: CHAPTER OUTLINE

### Chapter 1: The Hum (Quarter 1)
Molly arrives at the Academy. Player learns the daily loop, the Pressure Gauge, the track system (Greenwork locked). First bad Pressure day. The panic attack. The greenhouse. Lola. "Vapeurs don't work on me." "There's a lot that science doesn't explain." Ends with the gauge settling to green without Vapeur for the first time. 60-90 minutes.

### Chapter 2: Greenwork (Quarter 2)
Lola takes Molly to the Ninth for the first time. Molly begins her Greenwork minor. First Rootwork encounter — accidental, a flash of blue on the gauge. Alaric's concern deepens. The founding storm story heard in the Ninth. The lights flicker — the player might notice the correlation. 90-120 minutes.

### Chapter 3: The Architect's Mother (Quarter 3)
The grandmother revelation — however it comes (Thibodaux, Archives, medical records). Molly confronts Alaric about his mother. The mother's intuition crystallizes. The major switch becomes available. Pressure begins climbing and not coming back down. 90-120 minutes.

### Chapter 4: The Forecast (Quarter 4, interrupted)
Pressure rising relentlessly. Alaric pushing the harvester harder. The correlation between harvesting and Molly's suffering becomes visible. Lola takes Molly to the dome wall. The revelation — she sees the storm. The confrontation with Alaric. The storm hits. The triage begins. 90-120 minutes.

### Chapter 5: Second Line at the Edge of the World
The storm. The room. The choice. The four endings. The morning after. 90-120 minutes.

Total: 7-10 hours across five chapters.

## PART NINE: PRODUCTION NOTES

### Engine
Unity 2022.3 LTS. Sufficient for all systems — 2D illustrated scenes, UI-driven Pressure Gauge, branching dialogue, save persistence, audio layering.

### Dialogue System
Ink or Yarn Spinner for branching narrative. Don't build custom — use proven tools.

### Art Pipeline
Midjourney/Scenario.gg for initial compositions. Photoshop for refinement and consistency. Comic panels as premium story-moment art. Daily gameplay uses illustrated backgrounds and character portraits.

### Audio
Adaptive hum system with three layers mapped to Pressure. Ward-specific ambient music. New Orleans musicians for final soundtrack (budgeted in publisher ask).

### Save System
Serialize full game state — Pressure history, relationships, track progress, choices, dispatch history, journal entries, Vapeur usage, Rootwork level. Forward-compatible for episodic persistence.

### Chapter 1 Build Priority (MIT xPRO Capstone Target)
1. Pressure Gauge functional with zone response ✓
2. Daily cycle system (morning/afternoon/evening/sleep)
3. Dialogue system integrated (Ink or Yarn Spinner)
4. Track selection screen with locked Greenwork
5. 3-4 locations with background art
6. Character portraits (Molly, Lola, Alaric minimum)
7. Greenhouse scene playable with branching dialogue
8. Vapeur mechanic functional
9. Save system
10. Hum audio layer
11. Comic panel sequences for key scenes
12. Polish, testing, presentation

## PART TEN: WHAT THIS GAME IS ABOUT

Halcyon Academy is about what happens when the thing everyone tells you is wrong with you turns out to be the truest thing about you.

It's about a girl who hears a hum that nobody else hears, in a city that was built to keep the weather out, by a father who loves her in a language she can't speak.

It's about the difference between being treated and being seen.

It's about a Pressure Gauge that the player thinks measures anxiety but actually measures weather — and the moment that recontextualization changes everything.

It's about New Orleans. About a city that should not exist, that keeps getting rebuilt, that survives through stubbornness and music and the refusal to let the water win.

It's about two young women who find each other in a greenhouse and discover that neither of them is broken.

It's about three ways to answer a storm: with order, with love, or with surrender to something bigger than both.

It starts with a needle, a gauge, and a girl who hears the hum.

*Halcyon — from the Greek myth of the kingfisher bird that calms the seas to nest. Calm in the midst of storm.*

*The hum is real. It was always real. Build the game that explains it to the world.*
